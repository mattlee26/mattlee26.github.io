### Instructor

**PEP 809: Statistical Programming in R**, Massachusetts College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences (Spring 2026)

I designed and teach this 15-week online course for master's students in Pharmaceutical Economics and Policy. It covers data import and cleaning, `dplyr`, `ggplot2`, reshaping data, functions and iteration, R Markdown and Quarto, and Git/GitHub. Students also learn t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square tests, correlation, linear and logistic regression, and methods for survival, panel, and clustered data, and they finish with a capstone analysis of NHANES data.

### Teaching Fellow, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

**PHS 2000A/B: Quantitative Research Methods in Population Health Sciences** (Fall 2021, Spring 2022, Fall 2022)

This is the year-long core methods course for first-year PhD students in Population Health Sciences. It covers sampling, estimation and inference for regression models, model selection, survival and longitudinal analysis, measurement error, causal inference and mediation, and econometrics. I led weekly labs and office hours using my own slides and R examples, wrote and graded homework and exams, and helped revise the course when it moved online. My lab lectures covered regression, measurement error, survival analysis, causal interaction and mediation, factor analysis, and meta-analysis.

**SBS 203/204: Program Planning; Program Implementation and Evaluation** (Spring 2022, 2023, and 2024)

A required course for master's students in Social and Behavioral Sciences on community health needs assessment, program planning, implementation, and evaluation. I led class discussions, gave a lecture on cost-effectiveness analysis and economic evaluation, and gave feedback on students' needs assessments and CDC grant applications.

**NUT 202: The Biological Basis of Human Nutrition** (Spring 2022)

A required course for master's and doctoral students in Nutrition on how carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins, and minerals are metabolized, and how diet relates to heart disease, diabetes, obesity, hypertension, and cancer. I coordinated guest lectures, ran weekly review sessions, and gave a lecture on iron and copper metabolism.
