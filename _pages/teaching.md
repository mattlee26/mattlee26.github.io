### Instructor

**PEP 809: Statistical Programming in R** — Massachusetts College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences (Spring 2026)

A 15-week online graduate course I designed and teach for master's students in Pharmaceutical Economics and Policy. Topics include data import and cleaning, `dplyr` manipulation, `ggplot2` visualization, reshaping, functions and iteration, R Markdown/Quarto reporting, and Git/GitHub version control. Students apply t-tests, ANOVA, chi-square tests, correlation, linear and logistic regression, and survival, panel, and clustered-data methods in a capstone analysis of NHANES data.

### Teaching Fellow, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

**PHS 2000A/B: Quantitative Research Methods in Population Health Sciences** (Fall 2021, Spring 2022, Fall 2022)

Year-long core course for first-year PhD students in Population Health Sciences covering sampling, estimation and inference for regression models, model selection, survival and longitudinal analysis, measurement error, causal inference and mediation, and econometrics. I led lab sessions and office hours with original lecture slides and R applications (regression, measurement error, survival analysis, causal interaction and mediation, factor analysis, and meta-analysis), developed and graded assessments, and helped refine the course during the transition to virtual learning.

**SBS 203/204: Program Planning; Program Implementation and Evaluation** (Spring 2022, 2023, and 2024)

Required course sequence for master's students in Social and Behavioral Sciences on community health needs assessment, program planning, implementation, and evaluation, with an emphasis on community engagement and applied research. I facilitated discussion, gave a lecture on cost-effectiveness analysis and economic evaluation, and gave feedback on community health needs assessments and grant applications.

**NUT 202: The Biological Basis of Human Nutrition** (Spring 2022)

Required course for master's and doctoral students in Nutrition on the biochemistry and metabolism of macronutrients, vitamins, and minerals in the context of chronic disease. I coordinated guest lectures, led weekly review sessions, and gave a lecture on iron and copper metabolism.
