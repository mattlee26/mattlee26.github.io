My research looks at how food and social policies affect diet, food security, obesity, and cardiometabolic health, and whether they reduce health disparities. I mostly use quasi-experimental designs such as difference-in-differences and interrupted time series. I also analyze randomized trials, build microsimulation models for cost-effectiveness analysis, and sometimes use machine learning. I often work directly with government agencies and policymakers.

### Nutrition assistance programs

I study SNAP, WIC, and school meals, including how benefit amounts, remote services, and paperwork requirements affect participation, food security, and diet.

- Remote WIC services and remote benefit issuance increased participation and retention ([_JAMA Netw Open_ 2026](/publications/#kenney2026remote); [_Health Affairs_ 2026](/publications/#dore2026wic))
- Families redeemed most of the increased WIC fruit and vegetable benefit ([_Pediatrics Open Science_ 2026](/publications/#kenney2026increases))
- Statewide universal school meals and food insecurity ([_Pediatrics_ 2026](/publications/#kinderknecht2026statewide))
- The 2021 SNAP benefit increase, food security, and diet quality in Massachusetts ([_Nutrients_ 2026](/publications/#poole2026changes))
- Simpler SNAP recertification and uninterrupted access to benefits ([_JAMA Netw Open_ 2022](/publications/#kenney2022simplification))
- Caregivers' views on using WIC and SNAP together ([_AJPH_ 2025](/publications/#gago2025struggle))

### Cash transfers and anti-poverty policy

I study whether income support and paid leave improve health, and how to help eligible families claim benefits like the Earned Income Tax Credit.

- Unconditional cash transfers improved diet quantity and quality in a randomized study in Chelsea, MA ([_Nature Food_ 2026](/publications/#lee2026randomized))
- State paid family leave and perinatal health ([_Pediatrics_ 2026](/publications/#lee2026impacts))
- A rapid review of economic assistance models for food security and diet quality ([_Health Affairs_ 2025](/publications/#poole2025understanding))
- A randomized pilot of behavioral strategies to increase EITC take-up among California WIC participants ([under review](/publications/#lee2026behavioral))

### Sugar-sweetened beverage taxes and food marketing

I have evaluated local beverage taxes in Berkeley, Oakland, San Francisco, and Philadelphia, looking at consumption, prices, store stocking, and advertising.

- Consumption three years after the Berkeley tax ([_AJPH_ 2019](/publications/#lee2019sugar))
- Retail prices after the Oakland and San Francisco taxes ([_AJPH_ 2020](/publications/#falbe2020higher))
- Advertising and stocking at small retailers after the Philadelphia tax ([_AJPM_ 2024](/publications/#lee2024advertising))
- Beverage ad spending after the Philadelphia tax ([_AJPM_ 2024](/publications/#hua2024philadelphia))
- Socioeconomic disparities in outdoor beverage advertising ([_Prev Med Rep_ 2022](/publications/#zahid2022socioeconomic))

### Cost-effectiveness and microsimulation

I have used the Childhood Obesity Interventions Cost-Effectiveness Study (CHOICES) microsimulation model to estimate the long-term health effects and costs of proposed policies, and I developed the R code that processes the model's output.

- A statewide sugar-sweetened beverage excise tax in California ([_AJPM_ 2024](/publications/#lee2024sugar))
- An improved WIC food package to prevent childhood obesity ([_Pediatrics_ 2024](/publications/#kenney2024cost))

### Diet quality in children and adolescents

- Changes in Healthy Eating Index-2020 scores and BMI among adolescents ([_J Nutr_ 2026](/publications/#lee2026longitudinal))
- Trends in toddler diet quality, 1999 to 2018 ([_Pediatrics_ 2024](/publications/#zimmer2024trends))
- `dietaryindex`, an R package for calculating dietary pattern scores ([_AJCN_ 2024](/publications/#zhan2024dietaryindex))

### Work with local governments

As a postdoctoral fellow at the Taubman Center for State and Local Government at the Harvard Kennedy School, I evaluated city programs. In Los Angeles, I used BERT and other language models on unstructured call text to estimate how alternative emergency response affected behavioral health. In Detroit, I used mixed methods to study the causes of homelessness and to improve housing crisis call scripts and referrals for residents facing eviction.
