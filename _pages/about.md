Hi, I'm Matt (he/him). I'm a population health scientist, and I study how food and social policies affect diet, health, and health equity.

I'm a Project Leader for Research at [Partners for Public Good](https://www.partnersforpublicgood.org/). There I work on values-based food purchasing for schools and lead a study comparing how U.S. cities run public procurement. I also teach Statistical Programming in R as adjunct faculty in Pharmaceutical Economics and Policy at the [Massachusetts College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences](https://www.mcphs.edu/), and I consult for Kurtosis Research.

Most of my research evaluates programs and policies like SNAP, WIC, school meals, sugar-sweetened beverage taxes, paid family leave, and cash transfers. I use causal inference, econometric, epidemiologic, and simulation methods. In a randomized study in Chelsea, MA, we found that unconditional cash transfers improved how much and how well people ate ([_Nature Food_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026randomized)). In a national study, we looked at how state paid family leave policies affect perinatal health ([_Pediatrics_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026impacts)).

Before this, I was a postdoctoral fellow at the [Taubman Center for State and Local Government](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/taubman) at the Harvard Kennedy School, and at the Social Policies for Health Equity Research (SPHERE) Center at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. I have a PhD in Population Health Sciences (Public Health Nutrition) and an SM in Biostatistics from Harvard, and an MS in Epidemiology and a BA in Public Health from UC Berkeley.

When I'm not knee-deep in R code, I play the oboe and English horn, look for sunny hikes, and cook (and eat) new foods.
