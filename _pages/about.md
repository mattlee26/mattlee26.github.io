Hi, I'm Matt! (he/him) I'm a population health scientist who studies how **food and social policies** shape diet, health, and health equity, with the goal of informing interventions that are effective, equitable, and cost-effective.

I'm currently **Project Leader, Research** at [Partners for Public Good](https://www.partnersforpublicgood.org/), where I lead research on values-based public food procurement, from school food purchasing to benchmarking municipal procurement across the U.S. I'm also **adjunct faculty** in Pharmaceutical Economics and Policy at the [Massachusetts College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences](https://www.mcphs.edu/), where I teach statistical programming in R, and a research consultant with Kurtosis Research.

My work applies **causal inference, econometric, epidemiologic, and simulation-based methods** to evaluate policies such as nutrition assistance (SNAP, WIC, and school meals), sugar-sweetened beverage taxes, paid family leave, and unconditional cash transfers. Recent work includes a randomized evaluation showing that guaranteed income improved diet quantity and quality in Chelsea, MA ([_Nature Food_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026randomized)), and a nationwide quasi-experimental study of paid family leave and perinatal health ([_Pediatrics_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026impacts)).

Previously, I was a postdoctoral fellow with the [Government Performance Lab](https://govlab.hks.harvard.edu/) at the Harvard Kennedy School and with the Social Policies for Health Equity Research (SPHERE) Center at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. I hold a PhD in Population Health Sciences (Public Health Nutrition) and an SM in Biostatistics from Harvard University, and an MS in Epidemiology and a BA in Public Health from the University of California, Berkeley.

When I'm not knee-deep in R code, I play the oboe and English horn, look for the sunniest hikes I can find, and cook (and eat) new foods. 🍳 🍜 🥟
