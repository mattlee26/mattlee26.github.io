Hi, I'm Matt! I'm a population health scientist who studies how food and social policies affect diet, health, and health equity.

I'm currently a Project Leader in Research at [Partners for Public Good](https://www.partnersforpublicgood.org/). There, I lead a study to comprehensively examine and benchmark public procurement across U.S. cities using a combination of administrative, survey, and document data. In addition, I also support work on values-based food purchasing and projects assessing innovations being tested by state and local transit agencies. Separately, I teach Statistical Programming in R as adjunct faculty in Pharmaceutical Economics and Policy at the [Massachusetts College of Pharmacy and Health Sciences](https://www.mcphs.edu/).

The bulk of my research evaluates social programs and policies like SNAP, WIC, school meals, sugar-sweetened beverage taxes, paid family leave, and cash transfers, and their impacts on health and health behaviors. I use causal inference, econometric, epidemiologic, and simulation-based methods as a means of understanding and evaluating policy. Two of my recently-published studies include a randomized study in Chelsea, MA, where we found that unconditional cash transfers improved how much and how well people ate ([_Nature Food_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026randomized)), and an evaluation of
of state paid family leave policies and their impacts on perinatal health ([_Pediatrics_, 2026](/publications/#lee2026impacts)).

Prior to my current role, I was a postdoctoral fellow at the [Taubman Center for State and Local Government](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/taubman) at the Harvard Kennedy School, and at the [Social Policies for Health Equity Research (SPHERE) Center](https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/social-policies-health-equity-sphere/) at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. I hold a PhD in Population Health Sciences (Public Health Nutrition) and an SM in Biostatistics from Harvard, and an MS in Epidemiology and a BA in Public Health from UC Berkeley.
